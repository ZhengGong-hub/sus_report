Carbon-measure disclosure and the change in firm emissions

The outcome is the year-on-year change in log emissions rather than the level. Each specification is stated in full, then reported, then discussed.

1,647 companies2011–20246 outcomes152 regressionsrun: test_trucost

What is being estimated, and how to read it

The outcome is Δln(y)t = ln(yt) − ln(yt-1). A coefficient of −0.10 means emissions fell about 10 percentage points faster in years the measure is discussed — a difference in growth rate, not in level.

Levels and changes answer different questions

The disclosure indicators are stocks: once a firm has a power purchase agreement it keeps mentioning it every year. A level regression asks “are firms carrying this measure at a lower level?”; this one asks “are they trending down faster?”. A measure that permanently lowers emissions and is then discussed indefinitely appears in the difference only in its adoption year, and near zero thereafter. A null here is therefore not evidence against a level effect.

Differencing removes each firm’s permanent level, so the company fixed effect no longer absorbs a firm-specific level — it absorbs a firm-specific trend. Every specification is run four ways, crossing two pooling windows for the indicators with two timings:

  • Window — 1 year (that year’s report alone) or 3 years (pooled with the two before it, giving markedly more stable indicators).
  • Lag — 0 pairs disclosure with the same year’s change; 1 pairs it with the following year’s change, the ordering to expect if disclosure precedes abatement.

Both years must be positive to form the difference, so samples run roughly 4,100–5,200 company-years at lag 0 and 2,600–3,700 at lag 1. Market-based Scope 2 is far smaller at 610–635, and its lagged specifications fall below the minimum sample and are not estimated.

On the standard errors

Heteroskedasticity-robust, not clustered by company. Since each firm appears repeatedly this is likely too generous; clustering leaves coefficients unchanged but widens most standard errors. Read the p-values as a lower bound.

Comment on Part 1

Four measures separate from zero, and they divide neatly into two that behave as designed and two that do not.

Process emissions is the most consistent result in the run. On Scope 1 intensity it is −0.106 (s.e. 0.038) at the 1-year window and −0.098 (0.037) at three years, both contemporaneous, on 174–203 switching firms — firms discussing process-emissions measures see Scope 1 falling roughly 10 percentage points faster than their own trend. At lag 1 it fades to −0.044 and −0.003, which is what a one-off adoption-year effect looks like rather than a persistent trend difference.

General renewable electricity is negative on both Scope 2 measures at the 3-year window: −0.284 (s.e. 0.120) on market-based and −0.078 (0.035) on location-based. The market-based figure is the one renewable procurement is meant to move, and it carries much the larger coefficient — but on only 38 switching firms, against 364 for the location-based estimate. Treat the market-based number as suggestive at best.

Running the other way, on-site renewables is +0.091 (s.e. 0.035) on Scope 1 intensity at the 3-year window, contemporaneous, on 388 switching firms. Emissions grow faster in years firms discuss on-site generation. And power purchase agreements is +0.125 (s.e. 0.051) on location-based Scope 2 at lag 1 — the Scope 2 measure that does not credit contracts, so not the outcome a PPA acts on.

Energy efficiency is worth noting separately. It is the only measure showing the lagged pattern one would expect from a retrofit with a build-out period: nothing contemporaneously (+0.022, p = 0.59) and −0.151 (s.e. 0.062) a year later, on 329 switching firms. It appears only at the 1-year window; at three years the lagged estimate is −0.090 with p = 0.20.

Scope 3 downstream produces nothing below 5% at any window or lag.

Comment on Part 2

This is where the level and difference specifications diverge most, and it is worth being explicit.

In the level regressions the Scope 1 indicator was the strongest result we had — around −0.19 on Scope 1 emissions with p = 0.002. In first differences it is not distinguishable from zero in any of the four specifications: −0.026, −0.047, −0.042 and −0.041, with standard errors of 0.043–0.078 and p-values from 0.43 to 0.60. The sign is right in all four and the 3-year magnitude is about a fifth of the level estimate.

That is what a persistent level difference looks like once differenced away, so it does not contradict the level result. It does relocate it: the level finding rests on firms carrying a lower Scope 1 level while discussing Scope 1 measures, not on firms visibly bending their trend downward after they begin. That is a weaker basis for a causal reading.

Only three scope-level estimates clear 5% anywhere, and none is on its own scope: Scope 1 on location-based Scope 2 at lag 1 (+0.126, s.e. 0.049), Scope 3 upstream on Scope 3 downstream (+0.160, s.e. 0.076), and Scope 3 downstream on the monetised damage measure (+0.026, s.e. 0.013). All positive.

Comment on Part 3

The scope-matched results carry through largely unchanged when the other twenty-odd measures are added, which is expected: conditioning on sparse indicators that are mostly uncorrelated with the measure of interest moves little. Process emissions again appears on Scope 1 at both windows, and also on Scope 3 downstream.

The additional entries here are mostly measures landing on outcomes they do not act on — use-phase emissions and packaging on Scope 1, business travel on Scope 2. With thirty indicators against six outcomes and four timing combinations, some of that is unavoidable.

Comment on Part 4

On a differenced outcome the two specifications largely agree, which is itself the finding. Mean absolute coefficient is 0.048 with fixed effects and 0.063 without; explanatory power is 0.8% and 2.1%.

That contrasts sharply with the level specifications, where dropping the fixed effects moved coefficients by an order of magnitude — fuel switching went from +0.02 to +1.62, methane from +0.08 to +2.39 — because firm identity explained most of the variation in emission levels. Differencing already removes the firm’s permanent level, so it does much of the work the company fixed effect was doing. The fixed effects still matter, but they are no longer the difference between an interpretable estimate and a sector comparison.

Taking the four parts together

  • Process emissions is the one measure behaving as designed: scope-matched, right sign, consistent across both contemporaneous windows, roughly −10 percentage points on the Scope 1 growth rate, fading at lag 1 as a one-off adoption effect would.
  • Energy efficiency shows the expected lagged pattern — nothing contemporaneously, about −15 points a year later — but only at the 1-year window.
  • General renewable electricity is negative on both Scope 2 measures, largest on market-based, though that estimate rests on 38 firms.
  • On-site renewables and power purchase agreements both run positive, and the PPA estimate falls on the Scope 2 measure that does not credit contracts.
  • The scope-level Scope 1 indicator, the strongest level result, does not survive differencing — right sign throughout, a fifth the size, not separable from zero.

Read alongside the level specifications, most of what looked like a relationship in levels is a persistent difference between firms rather than a change that follows disclosure. Process emissions is the clearest exception, and energy efficiency the most suggestive.

Two things would sharpen this. Sector-by-year fixed effects would test whether the Scope 2 estimates reflect which firms sign renewable contracts rather than an effect of signing them. And hand-coding a sample of company-years against the extracted indicators would show how much of the imprecision is measurement rather than absence of a relationship.

Estimation. Outcome is Δln(y) formed from the Trucost frame’s own previous-year value, which is near-continuous, rather than from the panel, which holds only years the firm filed a report. Both years must be strictly positive. Fixed effects absorbed via within transformation (pyfixest); firms observed once are removed iteratively where fixed effects are present. Report length is controlled throughout. A lag replaces the contemporaneous indicators rather than joining them, and is matched on year explicitly so panel gaps never pair non-adjacent years.

Coverage. Scope 3 upstream is not used as an outcome: only about 11% of its values are firm-reported. Scope 2 market-based is reported only from 2020. Scope 1 is 69% firm-reported, location-based Scope 2 66%, Scope 3 downstream 41%.
