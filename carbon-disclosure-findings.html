Carbon-measure disclosure and firm emissions: regression results

Three specifications, differing only in how the disclosure measures are grouped on the right-hand side. Each is stated in full, then reported, then discussed.

1,647 companies2011–20247 outcomes48 regressionsrun: test_trucost

Common structure

All three parts estimate the same equation and differ only in what enters as x. The outcome is always logged emissions, so a coefficient of +0.10 means roughly 10% higher emissions in years the measure is discussed.

Company fixed effects mean we never compare one firm with another — only a firm with itself across years. Year fixed effects remove whatever moved all firms together in a given year. Report length is controlled throughout, because a measure can only be recorded if it appears in text we sampled.

Every specification is run twice, once for each way of building the disclosure indicators:

  • 1-year window — the indicator comes from that year’s report alone.
  • 3-year window — pooled over the report year and the two before it. Indicators built this way are considerably more stable (year-to-year correlation 0.77, against 0.39 at one year), so this is the specification to read as primary.
On the standard errors

This run uses heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors that are not clustered by company. Since each firm is observed repeatedly, that is likely too generous. Clustering by firm leaves every coefficient unchanged but widens about 85% of the standard errors. The p-values below should be read as a lower bound; nothing about the estimates themselves changes.

Part 1 — Measures matched to the scope they act on

The most focused specification. For each outcome, x holds only the measures that act on that emissions scope: on-site measures for Scope 1, electricity procurement for Scope 2, downstream measures for Scope 3 downstream. A measure is dropped when fewer than 30 firms change their disclosure of it, which is why Scope 3 downstream shows four measures rather than seven.

Comment on Part 1

Most estimates are small relative to their standard errors. The ones that stand out:

  • Power purchase agreements on location-based Scope 2: +0.183 (s.e. 0.043) at three years, the largest estimate in the study, and +0.059 (s.e. 0.039) at one year. Note the outcome — the location-based figure uses average grid intensity and does not credit contracts, so a procurement measure is not expected to move it.
  • General renewable electricity on the same outcome: −0.084 (s.e. 0.032). The expected direction, and the only scope-matched estimate pointing down with any precision.
  • On-site renewables on Scope 1 intensity: +0.074 (s.e. 0.033), on 398 switching firms.
  • Methane and fugitive emissions on absolute Scope 1: +0.177 (s.e. 0.080), but on only 47 switching firms — the thinnest identification here.

Energy efficiency is notable for the opposite reason. It has the most switching firms of any Scope 1 measure (554 at one year, 308 at three), yet the estimate is +0.015 (s.e. 0.037) at one year and −0.078 (s.e. 0.054) at three. It changes sign between windows and is imprecise in both.

Two outcomes produce nothing separable from zero. Market-based Scope 2 — the figure that does credit renewable contracts — has all four measures between −0.070 and +0.032 with standard errors of similar size; it also has much the smallest sample, roughly 1,200 company-years against about 5,000 elsewhere, since Trucost reports it only from 2020. Scope 3 downstream ranges from −0.136 to +0.034 against standard errors of 0.08–0.23.

Comment on Part 2

The clearest result in the exercise is here. The Scope 1 indicator is −0.186 (s.e. 0.059) on Scope 1 intensity at the 3-year window, and −0.178 (s.e. 0.061) on absolute Scope 1 — firms show roughly 17–19% lower Scope 1 emissions in years they discuss any Scope 1 measure than in their own other years. It carries through to the Scope 1 + tier-1 aggregate at −0.060 (s.e. 0.022), the attenuation one would expect given Scope 1 is only part of that aggregate.

Right sign, on the outcome the indicator is meant to act on, with 277 switching firms behind it. This is the only place in the study where those three things coincide.

It depends on the window. At one year the same coefficient is −0.055 (s.e. 0.042) — same direction, under a third the size, not separable from zero. Since the 1-year indicators are much noisier, attenuation at the shorter window is what one would expect if the 3-year estimate is real; but the gap is large enough to be worth treating carefully rather than assuming.

Elsewhere the pattern is harder to read. Scope 3 upstream is +0.039 (s.e. 0.015) on the aggregate outcome, Scope 3 downstream is +0.062 (s.e. 0.029) on location-based Scope 2, and carbon removal is −0.177 (s.e. 0.074) on Scope 3 downstream. In each case indicator and outcome belong to different scopes.

Comment on Part 3

Reading by measure rather than by outcome:

  • Power purchase agreements appear four times, always positive, across three outcomes — location-based Scope 2 (+0.173), absolute Scope 1 at both windows (+0.140, +0.122) and Scope 1 intensity (+0.114). Only one of those is an outcome a procurement contract acts on.
  • Technological carbon removal appears four times, always positive (+0.073 to +0.101), on 79–86 switching firms.
  • Voluntary offsets appear four times, always negative (−0.047 to −0.098).
  • Energy efficiency appears twice, both negative and sizeable (−0.154, −0.191) — but on Scope 3 downstream, not on Scope 1 where it acts.
  • Supplier engagement is negative on location-based Scope 2 at both windows (−0.093, −0.058).

Estimates here are generally larger than in Part 1 but rest on the same underlying variation, now spread across thirty simultaneous indicators. Several land on outcomes outside their own scope: use-phase emissions on absolute Scope 1, business travel on both Scope 2 measures, process emissions on Scope 3 downstream.

Taking the three parts together

  • Part 1 — scope-matched measures give small, imprecise estimates. The two clearest sit on location-based Scope 2 and point in opposite directions.
  • Part 2 — the coarser scope-level indicator produces the one result with the expected sign on its own outcome: Scope 1 disclosure against Scope 1 emissions, around −18% at the 3-year window.
  • Part 3 — with all thirty measures at once the estimates grow but scatter across outcomes, frequently landing where the measure does not act.

That Part 2 outperforms Part 1 is itself informative: pooling a scope’s measures into one indicator produces a cleaner signal than the individual measures do, which is consistent with the individual indicators being noisy proxies for whether a firm is doing something in that scope at all.

Two things would sharpen the picture. Sector-by-year fixed effects would test whether the Scope 2 estimates reflect which firms sign renewable contracts — their sector, or the grid they draw from — rather than an effect of signing them. And hand-coding a sample of company-years against the extracted indicators would show how much of the imprecision throughout is measurement rather than absence of a relationship.

Estimation. Fixed effects absorbed via within transformation (pyfixest). Company-years with zero or missing emissions are dropped, since the outcome is logged. Firms observed only once are removed iteratively. Disclosure indicators are contemporaneous with the outcome year — no lag is applied.

Coverage. Scope 3 upstream is not used as an outcome: only about 11% of its values are firm-reported, the rest being Trucost’s revenue-based model. Scope 2 market-based is reported only from 2020. Scope 1 is 69% firm-reported, location-based Scope 2 66%, Scope 3 downstream 40%.
